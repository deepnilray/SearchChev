\title{SearchChev on the real sky: a sealed validation and 29 new\\ periodic variable stars from ZTF}
\author{Deepnil Ray}

\begin{abstract}
The SearchChev detector was rebuilt to run entirely on real survey data: ZTF DR23 light curves read straight from the public HATS parquet archive and Gaia DR3 variability labels fetched by \texttt{source\_id} range. On four sealed sky tiles opened only after the model and threshold were frozen, it separates Gaia-classified variables from Gaia-quiet stars with AUC $0.954\pm0.008$ and a false-positive rate of $0.7\%$ (Wilson 95\% upper bound $1.0\%$); the full decision rule passes $9$ of $4000$ quiet stars ($0.23\%$) while keeping $63\%$ of the variables. A search of ten further tiles at $15^\circ\le|b|\le35^\circ$ scored $38{,}590$ of $110{,}011$ Gaia-quiet stars (G $=13$--$18.5$). Sixty survivors were vetted independently in every ZTF field and band; 37 passed, and a final check against VSX, the ZTF (Chen et al.\ 2020), ASAS-SN, ATLAS, Gaia DR3, TESS EB catalogues and SIMBAD removed three known stars and found one more only in an unvetted TESS target list. Of the remaining 34, five are set aside as possible systematics, leaving 29 graded A or B: 28 with no variability record in any of these sources and one previously present only in that unvetted list. Nine are graded A, including three detached eclipsing binaries with eclipses of 0.15--0.47 mag.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data}
\textbf{Light curves.} ZTF DR23 is served by IRSA as a HATS-partitioned parquet catalogue on S3 (\url{s3://ipac-irsa-ztf/contributed/dr23/lc/hats}). Each order-5 partition ($\approx3.4$ deg$^2$) was read with \texttt{pyarrow}, keeping $r$-band rows with at least 60 epochs and \texttt{catflags}$\,=0$.

\textbf{Labels.} A Gaia DR3 \texttt{source\_id} carries its level-12 nested HEALPix index in its top bits, $\lfloor \texttt{source\_id}/2^{35}\rfloor$, so the order-5 pixel $p$ is exactly the interval
\[
p\cdot4^{7}\cdot2^{35}\;\le\;\texttt{source\_id}\;<\;(p+1)\cdot4^{7}\cdot2^{35},
\]
and each tile needs a single indexed range query. Positives are stars in \texttt{vari\_classifier\_result} whose class ZTF can see (ECL, RR, CEP, T2CEP, LPV, RS, YSO, CV). Negatives are stars with no \texttt{vari\_summary} row, \texttt{phot\_variable\_flag} not VARIABLE and RUWE $<1.4$. Some negatives are unrecognised variables, so every false-positive rate below is an upper bound. ZTF objects were matched to Gaia within $1.5''$, requiring no other G$<18.5$ Gaia star within $3''$; where several fields observe a star, the light curve with most epochs is used.

\section{Detector and sealed validation}
Features are the 14 shape statistics of SearchChev (Lomb--Scargle significance, power, amplitude over error, reduced $\chi^2$, period, skew, IQR over error, von Neumann ratio, and six box-eclipse statistics). Magnitude, epoch count and baseline are excluded so the model cannot learn brightness. A gradient-boosted classifier was trained on eight tiles, magnitude-matched per 0.5 mag bin. The threshold was set from grouped out-of-fold scores at a 1\% false-positive target. Tiles were assigned train / validation / search by a seeded permutation written to disk before any download, and every access is logged against its phase.

One protocol change is recorded: after the first fit on training tiles only, 790 of 1599 positives were Gaia SOLAR\_LIKE stars, which pass the periodogram gate 6\% of the time in ZTF. The positive set was restricted to the classes above and refitted before any validation feature existed (\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.txt}).

The nine quiet stars that passed the full rule were vetted with the procedure of Section~\ref{sec:vet}. Eight showed their period in only one light curve, all from the same few field/CCD combinations, and one was an ATLAS candidate. The detector alone therefore lets through instrumental signals at the $0.2\%$ level, and the multi-light-curve vetting is what removes them.

\section{Search}
Ten search tiles were scanned (HEALPix order-5 pixels 1787, 3365, 236, 8116, 656, 5495, 1745, 10232, 1567, 1761). Targets were Gaia-quiet stars only, the population DPAC did not flag. Stars whose IQR over median error fell below the value that keeps 95\% of the sealed positives were skipped, which left 38{,}590 of 110{,}011. A star was kept if it passed the frozen threshold, the periodogram or eclipse gate, and recovered the same period (or a 1/2, 2 multiple, within 3\%) in each temporal half of its light curve. This gave 98 stars, of which 38 already had catalogue entries and 60 were new.

\section{Vetting}\label{sec:vet}
For every survivor, all ZTF DR23 rows within $2''$ in $g$, $r$ and $i$ and every field and CCD were analysed separately. A star passes when at least two independent light curves recover the period (to 1\%, allowing $\times\tfrac12$, $\times2$) at $\log_{10}\mathrm{FAP}<-3$ and no Gaia star brighter than G$+1$ lies within $8''$. The period was refined with a four-harmonic Lomb--Scargle over all light curves jointly. Each passing star was then rechecked against VSX, Chen et al.\ (2020) variables and suspects, ASAS-SN, ATLAS (Heinze et al.\ 2018), Gaia DR3 \texttt{vari\_classifier\_result}, TESS EBs (Prša et al.\ 2022), the TESS 10k EB catalogue and its unvetted target list (Kostov et al.\ 2025) at $5''$, and SIMBAD at $10''$. For a subset the full VizieR index at $5''$ was also queried; it returned only photometric, astrometric and stellar-parameter catalogues.

Of 60 new survivors, 37 passed. Three were found in catalogues on the recheck (a Chen et al.\ suspect, a TESS 10k EB, and an ATLAS + Chen suspect), because the in-loop query had silently returned empty on server errors; the cone query now treats any response without a VizieR resource header as an error. Two SIMBAD matches are plain stars (UCAC4 entries) with no variability classification.

\paragraph{Odd--even test.} For the two deepest eclipsing binaries the median depths in odd and even cycles at the listed $P$ agree within errors ($g$: $0.155\pm0.011$ vs $0.150\pm0.017$ and $0.454\pm0.025$ vs $0.488\pm0.031$ mag), and nothing is seen at phase 0.5. Either the orbital period is $2P$ with two near-equal eclipses, or the secondary eclipse is undetectably shallow; ZTF cannot separate these, and TESS or radial velocities will.

\section{What remains before submission}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Inspect PS1 / Legacy Survey cutouts of the 29 A and B stars for unresolved blends.
\item Pull TESS FFI light curves for the brighter A stars to fix eclipsing-binary periods ($P$ vs $2P$) and classes.
\item Submit A and B stars to VSX with ZTF light curves; the CSV and figures here hold every field needed.
\item Scale the search: ten tiles took four hours on two CPUs; the 2109 search tiles are embarrassingly parallel, and the yield of $\approx3$ vetted stars per tile implies several thousand over the full $|b|\ge15^\circ$ ZTF sky.
\end{enumerate}
Gaia DR4 (scheduled for 2 December 2026) will release epoch photometry and new variability classifications for these fields; the list above fixes the ZTF-based detections with their dates before that release.
